## 1. Positioning (from research)

**Market.** Nigeria has roughly 450–460 registered insurance brokers (NCRIB, 2023 onward). Most still run
on spreadsheets, paperwork and phone calls. Imported broker software rarely handles naira, local insurer
statements or Nigerian regulation.

**Why now.** The Nigerian Insurance Industry Reform Act 2025 (NIIRA) tightens the rules on brokers. It
reinforces "No premium, no cover", requires dedicated client accounts for premium money, and sets a
deadline for remitting premium to insurers. Late remittance carries interest above the CBN rate. A
brokerage that can't see, policy by policy, what it has collected and what it owes each insurer is
taking a real financial risk. *(Section numbers and day counts differ between summaries of the Act, so
the page doesn't quote a deadline. **[verify]** with legal before adding one.)*

**What iBroka is.** A cloud platform, hosted on Microsoft Azure, that keeps every client, policy, claim,
premium and remittance on the same record. It includes accounting, HR and Power BI reporting, with no
servers to buy. It has been in use with Nigerian brokers since 2019. **[verify]**

**Positioning statement.**
> For Nigerian insurance brokerages that still run on spreadsheets, iBroka is the brokerage platform
> that keeps every client, policy, premium and remittance on one record, so you renew on time, remit on
> time and always know what your book earns. Imported tools have to be bent to fit Nigerian broking;
> iBroka is built for it: naira, local insurers and the new reform act.

**Three audiences, one goal (get started).**

| Audience | What they care about | Where the page answers it |
|---|---|---|
| Brokerage owner / MD | Growth, visibility of the whole book, cost, regulatory risk | Hero, "For owners" tab, pricing |
| Operations lead | Policies, renewals, claims, documents, team workload | "For operations" tab, features 1–2 |
| Finance / accounts | Premium received, remittance due, commission, month-end | "For finance" tab, features 3–4 |

Primary action everywhere: **Start your free trial** (30 days, one user). Secondary: **See pricing** or
**See how it works** (scroll links, not competing buttons).

### 4. The problem
Heading: **Broking has outgrown the spreadsheet**

1. **Renewals slip.** When expiry dates live in a spreadsheet, nobody is told until the client calls.
2. **Remittance is now a legal deadline.** Under the 2025 reform act, premium you've collected has to reach
   the insurer on time, or interest is added. You need to know what you owe each insurer today.
3. **Month-end takes a week.** Matching receipts to policies, taking off commission and reconciling
   insurer statements by hand eats your finance team's time.

- **For operations:** Every policy from quote to renewal on one record. · Renewals flagged before they

- **For finance:** Premium matched to the policy as it comes in. · Commission taken off automatically,

## 4. Problems found in the pricing image

1. **The annual prices give one month free, not two.** Every annual price is exactly 11× the monthly
   price (₦14,607.33 × 11 = ₦160,680.63). "Pay one year and get two months free" would make the annual
   price 10× monthly: ₦146,073.30 / ₦231,282.80 / ₦438,220.00. Either the price or the copy needs to
   change.
2. **Kobo on marketing prices.** ₦14,607.33 reads like a currency conversion. Round figures
   (₦14,600) are easier to scan. Keep the exact figures if they're contractual.
3. **Button labels.** "TRY NOW" and "ORDER NOW" are all caps, and "order" suggests a physical product. The
   design system uses sentence case. Proposed: "Start free trial" and "Choose Plus".
4. **No recommended plan.** Highlighting one plan (for example Premium, "Most popular") usually helps
   people decide. Do you want one?
5. **Look.** The image uses different fonts and grey outline buttons. The page will use the design system:
   Instrument Sans, one teal primary button per card, and money in tabular figures.
